# Quotie closer script

Same steps as before. Talk like a normal person. Short sentences. No fancy words.

**Call:** Quotie Strategy Session  
**Job:** check how they quote, put a number on what it’s costing, shut the other options, walk the build, take the setup on this call  
**Not the job:** a feature tour, a “I’ll email you” close

**Time:** 35–50 min.

**Swap in:** `[_name_]` `[_trade_]` `[_pain_]` `[_what they want_]` `[_what changed_]` `[_what they use now_]` `[_quotes a month_]` `[_time per quote_]` `[_hours a week_]` `[_job worth_]` `[_plan_]`

Italics = notes.

---

## Before the call

### Headspace

1. Fresh call. Last one doesn’t come with you.
2. You’re on their side. Work out how they quote. Then decide if we should build it.
3. If it’s not a fit, say so.
4. Get numbers they’ll stand by. “A bit slow” doesn’t close.
5. Write down their words. You’ll say them back before you show the build.

### Two kinds of buyer

- **Bought software before / got burned:** they need to trust *you* and that we’ll actually build it. Show who does the work, and that we go back and forth until it’s right.
- **Still on Excel / Word / prices in someone’s head:** they’ve never bought this. Don’t rush the price. Make the other options look as expensive as they are.

### Money on this call

- Sell the plan that matches **who will send quotes**, not who’s on the phone. Two or more people = team plan. Don’t drop down to get a yes.
- Take the **setup on this call**. Monthly is after that.
- Don’t discount. Don’t make up a higher “was” price.
- If someone else has to say yes and they’re not on the call — don’t take “they’ll be right with it”. Get them on, or get “I can say yes”.
- Before you say the price they should have agreed: quoting is the problem, the other options don’t work, and if the build fits they can start today.

### Read first

Setter notes + the application. Have their quote open if they sent one.

- Who quotes, who should, how many, how long, hours a week, job worth
- Where it gets stuck, what they use, what changed, what they want
- Who says yes, can they pay, which plan
- Did they watch the video? Did they send a quote?

Proof you can use — nothing else:

- Jed Bolton, Bolton EC — $360k closed in one month once Quotie was dialled in. Customers picking from options on the quote.
- Benjamin Hughes, Hughes Electrical Group — solar, from yes through to install.
- Lachlan Williams, Coastal Cleans Co — he can see every quote, every day.

Don’t mention Virtue Roofing.

## 1. Intro (1–2 min)

### Open

Hey `[_name_]`, Locky from Quotie. How are you going?

I’ve got what Benji sent through, and `[_I’ve got the quote you sent / pull up a recent one if you can_]`. I’ve got a pen — have you got something to jot three steps later?

### Frame

I want a clear picture of how a quote actually gets done, what that’s costing you, and what it should look like.

I was a full-time estimator. I built the first version of this because owners were making the same job in Excel over and over. Buzz rebuilt the software. I still sit on the builds.

If I think we should do this, I’ll walk the whole thing — how we put your prices in, how your people use it, what it costs, and how we’d start. If we shouldn’t, I’ll say so.

Sound fair?

### First question

Benji had `[_how many quotes, how long, sitting on who, stuck where_]`. In your words — what’s actually going wrong with quoting?

Then ask about the work, not their feelings:

- What do you mean, in the steps?
- Where does that show up — getting the info, doing the numbers, making the quote, sending it, chasing it?
- How long has it been like that?
- *(shut up)*

## 2. Check their numbers (5 min)

Don’t skip this because Benji already asked. Check, then go one layer deeper.

I talk to a lot of owners who want `[_faster / look better / off my plate_]`. That’s different in every business.

In yours, is the main thing: quotes can’t go out without you… the numbers come out different… they go out too late… or nothing gets chased?

### How it runs

How many quotes a month, really?

How long is a normal one, counting the back and forth, until the customer has it?

Who touches it?

How many people need to send a quote once this is working?

What’s a normal won job worth?

Do you know roughly how many quoted jobs you win?

What are you using now — `[_Excel / Word / ServiceM8 / Fergus / a mix_]`?

### What’s broken

I don’t want to build you a nicer version of the same problem.

What do you actually want gone — doing it twice, you having to check every one, different numbers, slow to the customer, or quotes that just sit?

## 3. Get the real cost

One thing at a time. Get a number or a range. Then say it back.

### It all goes through them

What still can’t go out without you?

Walk a quote from “can I get a price” to send. Where do you have to touch it?

If you’re on the tools or away for a week, what happens to quotes?

If you hired a salesperson tomorrow, what would they quote in — and who would still have to check it?

### Time

Hours a week, all up, making quotes?

At that many quotes, that’s about `[_quotes × time_]` a month. That sound right?

What does that time come out of — paid jobs, selling, running the business?

If you had those hours back, what would they go into that isn’t getting done now?

### The numbers don’t match

If two people quoted the same job this week, would the price match?

Have you won a job and later found it was quoted too cheap? What was that worth?

Where do extras get missed?

### Slow to the customer

If a quote goes out tomorrow instead of today, what happens to the job?

How many quotes a month do you know you lost, or never heard back on, because they were late or looked like a basic Word doc?

What’s one of those jobs worth?

### Nobody’s chasing

How do you know what’s sitting there today?

Who chases them?

How many quotes are sitting with no next step?

Of those, how many would still be alive if someone rang this week?

### Say it back. Out loud.

So: about `[_n_]` quotes a month, `[_time_]` each, sitting on `[_who_]`, stuck at `[_where_]`.

That’s about `[_hours_]` a month on quotes.

Jobs are around `[_value_]`. When this is slow or the numbers are off, you’re looking at `[_jobs / $ they already said_]`. Plus whatever you give away when two quotes don’t come out the same.

Have I got that right?

*(If they change a number, use theirs. You price against this.)*

### Shut the other options

They have to say why the obvious fix doesn’t work. You’ll use that later.

**Just keep doing it**  
If it’s costing you `[_hours / it all goes through you / lost jobs_]`… why isn’t the answer just keep doing what you’re doing?

**Hire someone**  
What does a decent estimator or salesperson actually cost you, before they can send a quote the same way every time?  
And when they start — what do they quote in? If the prices are still in your head, you’ve paid a wage that still comes back to you.

**Set up Fergus / Jobber / ServiceM8 this week**  
What’s stopped that already being done?  
*(They’ll say: no time, generic, they become the admin, they tried.)*  
So you don’t need another login. You need someone to build how you actually price, and keep it right. That’s the job.

**Make a better Excel**  
You’ve had Excel the whole time. What’s stopped that being the answer?

**We’re busy enough**  
If this isn’t costing you hours, jobs, or the chance to get someone else sending quotes — we shouldn’t be on this call. Which one is it?

Stay here until they’ve shut their own exits.

## 4. Why now

How long have you known this was a problem? *(Just the time.)*

What changed in the last month or two that put it on the list now — more work, hiring, someone leaving, a lost job, a new type of work, you can’t put another person on quotes?

**If they stay vague**

I only take this on when there’s a real reason to do it now. Not “we should get organised”. What’s the reason in your business?

**If they’re still soft**

If we don’t change this in the next few months, what stays true — quotes still can’t go out without you, you’re still at `[_hours_]`, or you still can’t put someone else on it?

That’s costing you now, or you’ll live with it?

Don’t sell a “we’ll get to it”.

## 5. Why not just do it themselves (1–2 min)

You’ve clearly built a `[_trade_]` business.

Why haven’t you just grabbed another tool and built the quotes yourself?

*(They’ll say: no time, tried, it becomes another job, the last one died. That’s step 2 of the pitch.)*

## 6. What they’ve already tried

I don’t want this to feel like the last quoting thing you looked at.

What have you already used or looked at — Excel, ServiceM8, Fergus, hiring, a VA — and why wasn’t it the answer?

If they used something:

- Did customers actually get quotes out of it, or did you just look at it?
- Who was meant to set the quotes up?
- Why did it stop?

If they haven’t:

- What stopped you spending time on it?
- What about us building it is different from buying another tool?

`[_Need: last time died because they had to build it themselves_]`

## 7. If there’s a red flag *(only if you need it)*

Use for: “we bought software and didn’t use it”, “I need my partner”, “not sure we’ll sit down for this”, “we’ve been meaning to do this for years”.

`[_red flag_]` is how this dies. Not the software. The business never gives it a window.

### Someone else has to say yes

If the other owner isn’t on the call:

Who else has to say yes to a setup this size?

Are they in the quoting, or only the money?

If they have to say yes — let’s get them on now, or book 20 minutes with both of you this week while this is still fresh. I don’t want you having to explain it from memory.

If you can say yes on your own: say that clearly. I’ll hold you to it when we get to the number.

### Tie it down

If you want `[_what they want_]`, the business has to pay to get it built, and give us the real prices. Using it halfway is how you end up back in `[_what they use now_]`.

Are you happy to do both if this fits?

Based on what you’ve walked me through — `[_their numbers_]` — we can do this. *(pause)*

The reason I say that is `[_where it gets stuck, and how you’d lock that in_]`.

I’ll show you how we get from how you quote now to `[_what they want_]`.

Still got the pen? Write 1, 2 and 3 with a bit of space.

Three parts. Built around how you already price. Not a generic template.

## 9. The build — slow down

### Step 1 — We put how you already quote into the form

Write that down. We put how you already quote into the form.

Most tools fail because `[_their words: they had to build it / it was generic / it still sat on them_]`. That’s a job nobody in the business has time for. Fair?

We don’t ask you to become the software person. We sit with you and work out how you already price — products, labour, margins, extras, the maths you use.

We don’t change how you price. We lock what you already do into the form.

Then we build that form. The right options. The maths done. The person quoting fills it in. They don’t make a number up.

We build the quote so it looks like you — cover, scope, options, terms. Same every time.

That’s step 1. What are your thoughts?

### Step 2 — We get your people using it

Write that down.

Quoting software dies when nobody makes sure the first ten quotes come out right.

We don’t hand you a login and leave. Setup is the form, the quote, the emails, the pipeline, and hooking up what you already use — Gmail or Outlook on every plan, GoHighLevel and ServiceM8 on the team plan.

Then we sit with the people who will send quotes until they can do one without you checking every line.

Most businesses are sending real quotes in the first week. It’s not a one-day setup. We go back and forth until it matches how you work.

That’s why not to do it yourselves — you already said `[_their words from section 5_]`.

What are your thoughts?

### Step 3 — You run it. We change prices when you need it.

Write that down.

Day to day: fill in the form, hit generate, send. Under 30 seconds once it’s live.

Up to four options on a quote, so the customer picks instead of you rewriting it.

They can look at it online, sign, or say no and tell you why. You can see when they open or download it. Send from your own Gmail or Outlook. Replies come back in.

Quotes that are still out sit in a follow-up list. You can see draft, sent, won, lost. When you win, you can turn it into a job.

You don’t build templates after that. Price change or a new product — you come back to us for that change.

Does that match how you want this to run?

Those are the three steps.

## 13. Common pushbacks

One at a time. Answer. “Was that the thing?” Back to the invoice.

### “I need to think about it”

That’s fine. I don’t want a bad decision.

I also don’t want “think” to mean we didn’t finish.

What’s actually open — the number, cash this month, whether the team will use it, or whether we can match how you price?

*(Handle that. Then:)*  
If that’s clear, do you need time, or is it a yes or no on getting it built?

### “I need to talk to my partner”

Are they in the quoting, or only the money?

If they have to say yes: let’s get them on for 15 minutes now, or a time in the next two days while this is still clear. I’ll run the same numbers. I won’t ask you to sell it for me.

If you can say yes yourself: say that, and we go.

### “It’s a lot of money”

Against what — a monthly app, or against `[_hours a month / what a job is worth / a hire who still can’t quote without you_]`?

Setup is us building it. Monthly is less than a day of the time you already put into quotes.

I’m not going to call it cheap. I’ll put it next to what you just walked me through. Which bit is the issue — the setup, the monthly, or both?

### “Can we start smaller / just try it?”

A half build puts you back in Excel. If we don’t put how you actually quote into the form, there’s nothing to try.

We can do the one-person plan if only you will send quotes. We can’t do “a template to play with”. That’s not what we sell.

### “We’ve already got ServiceM8 / GHL / Fergus”

Good. We don’t rip those out. They hook up on the team plan. They’re average at quoting. That’s the gap.

Do you want quoting to stay in `[_what they use now_]`, or do you want quoting fixed and those tools to keep doing what they’re good at?

### “I’ll get my guy to set something up”

You can. That’s how this stays a side job for you or “the computer person”.

The hard part isn’t the software. It’s the form, the weird jobs, the look of the quote, and the team actually using it. That’s what you’re paying us to do.

### “Bad time of year / after this job”

This doesn’t get cheaper after the busy bit. You just do the same hours for longer.

If we start now, you’re sending quotes in it in a week. What’s the real hold-up — money this month, or time to sit with us on the prices?

If it’s money this month and everything else is a yes:  
`[_Only if you’ve agreed a split_]` we can take `[_amount_]` today to lock the week, rest before you go live. We don’t start on a promise.

### “The team won’t use it”

They won’t use an empty tool. They will use a form that matches how they already talk about a job. That’s the training, and that’s why the form is yours.

Who would push back, and what would they be pushing back on — a new way, or more admin than they have now?

### “What if it doesn’t match how we quote?”

Then we’re not finished. We keep going until it does. That’s included.

If we can’t match how you price, I don’t take you on. That’s why I wanted a real quote in front of us.

### “Do you do invoices / take payment / send texts?”

No. We don’t invoice. We don’t take deposits. We don’t send texts.

If that’s the main problem, I’m the wrong company. Is quoting the thing you want fixed?
