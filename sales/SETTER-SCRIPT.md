# Quotie setter script

Same steps as before. Say it like a normal person. Short sentences. No fancy words.

**Job:** see if they’re a real business, put numbers on the quoting problem, check they can pay, book Locky, make sure they show up.

**Not the job:** sell the whole product, talk price for 10 minutes, close the deal.

**Time:** 8–15 min. Already booked: 4–7.

**Swap in:** `[_name_]` `[_trade_]` `[_pain_]` `[_what they want_]` `[_closer_]` (Locky) `[_date_]` `[_time_]`

Black = say it. Italics = notes.

---

## Before you dial

Open the Close lead. Check:

- Just opted in, or they already applied?
- Already booked?
- Trade, quotes a month, how long a quote takes, what’s annoying them, when they want it, money — if they applied
- Leave the ad links as they are

You’re checking if we should build this for them. Not filling a calendar.

If they already said **NO** they can’t pay — don’t book them.

### Write these down before you hang up

Locky uses this on the next call.

- Who writes the quotes now, and who should
- How many quotes a month
- How long each one takes
- What’s a normal job worth
- Where quotes get stuck
- What changed lately
- Who says yes to the money
- Can they pay if it’s a fit — YES / MAYBE / NO

A range is fine. You don’t need perfect books.

## 1. Intro

Pick one.

### A. They only left their details

Hey, is this `[_name_]`?

Hey `[_name_]`, it’s Benji from Quotie. You put your details in about quoting in the business.

I just wanted to see if this is even relevant, and if it’s worth a chat with the guy who builds these. Did you watch the short video, or have you been flat out?

- **Watched it** → “Nice. What stood out for how you quote now?” Then Frame.
- **Haven’t** → “All good. I only need a few minutes to see if you should spend any more time on it.” Then Frame.
- **“What is this?”** → “We build a quoting system around how you already price jobs, so you’re not making every quote from scratch. I just want to see if that’s even a problem for you before I send you anywhere.” Then Frame.

### B. They applied, not booked

Hey `[_name_]`, Benji from Quotie. I saw your application come through.

I wanted to make sure we got it right, and see if it’s worth a call with Locky. Got a few minutes?

→ Frame, then go quicker. You already have their answers. Don’t ask the form again.

### C. Already booked

Hey `[_name_]`, Benji from Quotie. You’ve got a call with `[_closer_]` on `[_date_]`.

I just want to make sure that time is useful — check a couple of things, and what Locky needs from you. Make sense?

→ Don’t start from scratch. Check the numbers, why now, what they’ll send, then lock the show.

## 2. Frame

I just want to understand how you quote now, what it’s costing you, and if we can even help.

If it looks like a fit, I’ll get you in front of Locky. He’s the estimator. He builds these. He’ll walk you through how it would work in your business, and what it costs.

If it’s not a fit, I’ll tell you. No point wasting the call.

So what made you put your details in? What’s going wrong with quoting right now?

*(Stay here until they name a real problem. “We want to be more efficient” is not enough.)*

---

## 3. How they quote now

Do these in order.

1. **Problem** — is quoting actually messy, slow, or stuck on them?
2. **Cost** — hours, jobs, money. Numbers.
3. **What they want** — who should send quotes, how fast, same numbers
4. **Why now** — what changed in the business. Not a life story.

### How it works today

What does the business do?

Who writes the quotes right now — you, a salesperson, an estimator, the office, or a mix?

Once this is working, how many people need to send a quote? Just you, or a few of you?

*(If 2 or more, that’s the team plan. Write it down. Don’t let the next call sell them the one-person plan.)*

Roughly how many quotes go out a month?

Walk me through one. Someone asks for a price — who grabs it, where do the numbers come from, who checks it, how does the customer get it?

How long is that, start to finish — from “we should quote this” to the customer having it? Counting the back and forth.

What do you use now — Excel, Word, ServiceM8, Fergus, a bit of everything?

### The main problem

What’s the one part of that that’s hurting the business most?

**Get more**

When you say `[_pain_]`, that means different things to different people. In your week — where does a quote actually get stuck? Getting the info, doing the numbers, making the document, you checking it, sending it, or chasing it?

**If it’s still fluffy**

A lot of `[_trade_]` owners say they want quotes out faster. A lot of the time the real issue is the prices live in one person’s head, so nothing can go out without them. Is that you? Or is it something else — different numbers, slow to the customer, quotes just sitting there?

### What it costs

How many hours a week, all up, go into quotes — everyone who touches them?

Whose time is that — yours, a salesperson, office?

What’s a normal job worth when you win it?

Of the quotes you send, roughly how many come back as a yes? A range is fine.

If a quote goes out a day late, what happens to that job — you still get it, it goes quiet, or you never hear?

How many quotes are sitting there right now that nobody is chasing?

If two people quoted the same job this week, would the price come out the same?

**Say it back. Don’t skip this.**

So I’ve got about `[_n_]` quotes a month, `[_time_]` each, sitting on `[_who_]`, jobs around `[_value_]`, and it gets stuck at `[_where_]`. That’s roughly `[_hours_]` a month on quotes. And when it’s slow or the numbers are off, you’re looking at `[_jobs / $ if they said it_]`. Have I got that right?

*(If they fix a number, use theirs. Locky uses this bit.)*

### What they’ve already tried

Have you tried to fix this — a template, other software, hiring, a VA, a salesperson with nothing to quote in? What happened?

Who was meant to set that up and keep it going?

### Why now

You’ve been quoting this way for a while. What changed in the last month or two that made this a now problem, not a later problem?

Listen for: more work coming in, hiring or about to, someone leaving, a job lost because the quote was late or messy, new type of work, they can’t put a new person on quotes because that person still has to come back to them.

**If they say “we should get organised”**

We only take people on when there’s a real reason to do it now. More work, hiring, a lost job, someone leaving. What’s the one for you?

**If they’re still vague**

If we didn’t talk again, and quoting stayed exactly like this for the next few months — what does that cost you? Time, jobs, or you still can’t get anyone else sending quotes?

Okay. And that’s why this is on the list now? Or is it still a “we’ll get to it”?

*(If it’s “we’ll get to it” and it’s not costing them anything — don’t book.)*

### What they want

If quoting worked how it should — who could send a quote, how fast would it go out, and would the numbers come out the same every time?

That’s what Locky is building toward. Not a prettier PDF for the sake of it.

## 4. Can they say yes

Who can say yes to the money, then can they pay. Don’t book someone who can’t do either.

### Who says yes

If this is the right thing, who says yes to getting it built — you, a business partner, someone else?

- **Just them** → keep going.
- **Them and someone else** → “That call only works if they’re on it, or you’ve already got the go-ahead if the numbers and the build are right. Which one is it?”
  - Get the other person on the booking, or get “I can say yes”.
  - If they can’t, book a time both can do. Don’t send one person in to “have a look”.

### Money

So I send the right people to the right call.

This isn’t a cheap monthly app you set up yourself. We build the quoting system around how you already price — the form, the prices, the look of the quote, and we show your team how to use it. There’s a one-off to get it built, then a monthly after that.

One person and a team are different numbers. Locky will put the right one in front of you.

If it checks out and it’s clearly the right thing for the business — can you pay to get it built?

*(Ask it calmly. Let them talk.)*

### If they give you a number

Cool. And that’s money the business can actually put in this month if it’s right? Not a number that still needs another meeting?

### If they say “how much is it?”

Two prices, GST in.

- One person quoting: $5,800 to set it up, $495 a month
- Two to five people: $7,800 to set it up, $695 a month

The setup is us building it. The monthly is the software after that. No fee per quote.

I don’t need you to decide that with me. I need to know the business can do the setup if Locky shows you it matches how you already quote. Can it?

### If they ask again

If this was just an app I’d send you a link. It’s not. The call is to see if we can put how you already price into a form. If we can, and it’s the right plan — can the business pay for the setup?

### If they say a few hundred / they wanted cheap software

That’s a different thing. We don’t hand you an empty tool. If the budget is a cheap monthly login, I won’t put you in front of Locky.

→ Don’t book.

### If they say Maybe

That’s a normal answer before you’ve seen it against how you quote. The call is to decide if it’s worth it. If Locky doesn’t think we should do it, he’ll say so. You happy to have that chat with the person who can say yes?

## Don’t book

- Can’t get the person who pays on the call, and they can’t say yes themselves
- They want to build it themselves
- The real problem is invoices, taking money, or texts
- Hard no on paying
- Nothing’s wrong and they’re just looking
- They want a cheap monthly login and that’s it
