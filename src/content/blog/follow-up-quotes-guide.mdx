You sent the quote. The client went quiet. Now what?

Some businesses never follow up. Others try once and give up. A small number follow up systematically and consistently win jobs their competitors thought were dead.

The difference isn't luck. It's process.

## Why Clients Go Quiet

It's usually not what you think:

- **They're not ready yet.** Waiting on a partner's approval, another quote, a finance decision. They haven't forgotten you.
- **Your quote got buried.** 40 other emails pushed it down within 24 hours. They meant to get back to you.
- **They're avoiding an awkward "no."** A gentle follow-up actually gives them a graceful exit.
- **They have questions they didn't ask.** Something confused them, life got busy, and they never circled back.

You're not being pushy by following up. Most of the time, you're being helpful.

## The Timeline

**Day 2-3:** Short message. "Just checking the quote came through. Any questions?" Not a sales pitch. A service touchpoint.

**Day 7-10:** Slightly more substantive. Acknowledge they're busy, mention if your schedule for their preferred timeframe is filling up (only if true).

**Day 21:** Final systematic follow-up. "I'm following up one last time before I close this out. If the timing isn't right or you've gone another direction, totally fine. If you're still interested, let's get it booked."

**Day 45+:** For high-value jobs, a casual check-in every 4-6 weeks can revive dead leads. Keep it very brief.

## What Actually Works

**Keep it short.** Three sentences is enough. They've already seen the quote. They need a reminder and an easy way to respond.

**Make it about them.** "I wanted to make sure you had everything you needed" beats "I wanted to follow up on our quote."

**Be honest about urgency.** If your schedule genuinely has a constraint, say so. If you're making it up, don't. Clients can tell.

**Never apologise for following up.** "Sorry to bother you again" signals that you think your own message is an imposition. It's not. Be friendly, be confident.

## Example Messages

**Day 2-3:**
> Hi [Name], just checking in to make sure the quote came through. Let me know if you have any questions or want me to adjust anything.

**Day 7-10:**
> Hi [Name], following up on the quote from last week. Our schedule for [month] is starting to fill, so wanted to check if you're still keen to get this sorted. Happy to talk through anything.

**Day 21:**
> Hi [Name], last check-in from me on this one. If the timing isn't right or you've decided to go another direction, no stress at all. If you're still interested, let's get it booked in.

## Make It Systematic

The reason most trades businesses don't follow up consistently isn't laziness. It's that tracking 15 open quotes, remembering where each one sits in the timeline, and knowing who needs a follow-up today is genuinely hard to do manually.
